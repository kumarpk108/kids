The Auspicious Birth (1896)	On September 1 1896 during the Nandotsava festival celebrating Lord Krishna's birth Abhay Charan De was born in a small mud-walled house in the Tollygunge district of Calcutta[citation:6]. His father Gour Mohan De and mother Rajani named him Abhay meaning "fearless" hoping he would become a great devotee of Krishna. The neighborhood was filled with the sounds of kirtana as people celebrated the Lord's appearance.
Early Lessons in Devotion	As a young boy Abhay would daily visit the Radha-Govinda temple across from his home[citation:6]. He would climb onto the altar and ring the bells even before the priests arrived. His father seeing this encouraged him and gave him a small mridanga drum. Abhay would sit before the Deities and sing the bhajans his father taught him.
Childhood Pastimes with Subidhi	Abhay's closest childhood friend was Siddhesvar Mullik whom he called "Subidhi"[citation:6]. They shared a perambulator as infants and grew up like brothers. Together they would chant Hare Krishna and enact pastimes of Lord Krishna. This friendship taught Prabhupada the value of saintly association from the very beginning of his life.
Refusing the Chicken Broth	When young Abhay fell seriously ill with typhoid the family physician Dr. Bose prescribed chicken broth as medicine[citation:6]. Abhay remembering the promise he had made to his mother to never eat meat or fish refused firmly. He told his father he would rather die than break his vow. Gour Mohan De respected his son's determination and Abhay recovered through other means.
The College Years	Abhay attended the prestigious Scottish Churches College in Calcutta where he studied philosophy and economics[citation:6]. While his classmates were absorbed in Western thought Abhay remained fixed on Krishna consciousness. He would argue with professors about the superiority of Vedic knowledge and often invited friends to hear about Lord Chaitanya's teachings.
Meeting His Spiritual Master	In 1922 at the age of twenty-six Abhay Charan De met his spiritual master Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura for the first time[citation:6]. The meeting took place at the Gaudiya Matha in Calcutta. Without any formal introduction Abhay approached the great acarya who immediately instructed him to preach Krishna consciousness in English to the Western world.
The Order That Changed History	Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati told the young Abhay "You are educated young man. Why don't you preach Lord Chaitanya's message in English to the Western world?"[citation:6] Abhay objected saying he was a householder with responsibilities. The great acarya replied "If you are sincere Krishna will help you." This order remained in Prabhupada's heart for the next forty-three years.
Starting Back to Godhead	In 1944 Prabhupada began writing and publishing Back to Godhead magazine single-handedly[citation:6]. He would write the articles edit the content and even sell the copies on the streets of Calcutta. The magazine explained Vedic philosophy in clear English. Each issue cost only a few annas but its message was priceless—Krishna consciousness for the modern world.
The League of Devotees	Moving to Jhansi in 1953 Prabhupada started the League of Devotees an organization for spreading Krishna consciousness[citation:6]. He gave lectures distributed his magazines and initiated his first disciple Acarya Prabhakar Misra. Though the League had few members Prabhupada worked tirelessly maintaining the center through his own funds and determination.
The Vision of Vrindavana	In 1954 Prabhupada left family life and moved to the holy dhama of Vrindavana[citation:6]. He took residence at the historic Radha-Damodara temple where the great acaryas Rupa Goswami and Jiva Goswami had once lived. Here surrounded by the dust of Vrindavana he began the serious work of translating the Srimad Bhagavatam into English.
Garlanded by Krishna	One Janmastami night in Jhansi while performing kirtana alone in the temple room Prabhupada experienced a direct vision of Lord Krishna[citation:9]. He began dancing ecstatically wearing a garland of kadamba flowers the size of tennis balls that suddenly appeared around his neck. When his disciple Acarya Prabhakar asked where the garland came from Prabhupada replied "Krishna appeared and gave me this garland."
Writing in the Samadhi	For years Prabhupada lived in a small room at the Radha-Damodara temple writing his translations of the Bhagavatam[citation:6]. He would sit for hours at a small desk writing with a nib pen by candlelight. The room was his samadhi shrine his study and his kitchen all in one. Here despite poverty and poor health he produced the first three volumes of his life's work.
The Three Heart Attacks	While living in Vrindavana Prabhupada suffered three severe heart attacks[citation:6]. Each time he refused to go to the hospital saying he would depend on Krishna. His Godbrother Srila Sridhara Maharaja sent a doctor who prescribed medicine. Even in this condition Prabhupada continued writing. He would rest for a few days then return to his desk determined to complete the translation.
Preparing for the West	By 1965 at age sixty-nine Prabhupada felt his time was short[citation:6]. He had translated three volumes of the Bhagavatam but the order to preach in the West remained unfulfilled. He prayed to Krishna for an opportunity. Through the kindness of Sumati Morarji owner of Scindia Steamship Company he received free passage on a cargo ship called the Jaladuta meaning "Sea Messenger."
Boarding the Jaladuta	On August 13 1965 Prabhupada boarded the MV Jaladuta at the Calcutta port[citation:6]. He carried with him a trunk of his books some dry rations and forty rupees. His son Vrindavan and a few friends came to see him off. As the ship pulled away from the shore Prabhupada stood at the railing chanting Hare Krishna knowing he might never see India again.
The Diary of Sea Sickness	The first days on the Jaladuta were brutal for Prabhupada[citation:6]. He suffered terrible sea sickness with vomiting and dizziness. He wrote in his diary "I felt uncomfortable the whole day and night." The ship tilted and rolled on the rough waters of the Bay of Bengal. Yet even in this condition he continued chanting and reading his Bengali Caitanya-caritamrta.
Janmastami on the Ship	On August 20 1965 the Jaladuta was sailing in the Arabian Sea when Prabhupada celebrated Janmastami[citation:6]. He arranged a small meeting on the ship and spoke for an hour about Lord Krishna's appearance to all the officers. They distributed prasadam together. The captain was so pleased that he radiographed the news to Sumati Morarji in Bombay.
The Poem of Prayer	On the Jaladuta Prabhupada wrote a famous Bengali poem praying to Lord Krishna[citation:6]. He admitted his unworthiness and begged for strength to carry out his spiritual master's order. He wrote "I am a most fallen soul. But I have taken shelter of Your lotus feet." This prayer became the cry of his heart throughout his difficult first months in America.
Arriving in Boston	The Jaladuta arrived at the Boston harbor on September 17 1965 after thirty-five days at sea[citation:6]. Prabhupada was weak and seasick having eaten almost nothing for days. He stepped onto American soil for the first time wearing an old coat over his dhoti. He had no money no friends and no place to stay only the order of his spiritual master and faith in Krishna.
The First American Days	Prabhupada traveled from Boston to New York by bus carrying his trunk of books[citation:6]. He stayed briefly with a friend's family in Butler Pennsylvania then moved to New York City. He had no followers no temple and no income. He would walk the streets of Manhattan chanting japa wondering how he would ever fulfill his mission in this strange land.
Taking a Room on the Bowery	Prabhupada eventually found a small room on the Bowery a rough area of New York filled with homeless and drunk men[citation:6]. The room was dirty and cramped but it was cheap. Here he continued writing and translating. He would go out each day with a small harmonium singing the Hare Krishna mantra on street corners hoping someone would listen.
The Matchless Gift	One day while sitting alone in his Bowery room Prabhupada had a realization that he called "the matchless gift"[citation:6]. He understood that the Hare Krishna mantra was so powerful that even if chanted without perfect understanding it would deliver the chanter. This gave him confidence to continue his street sankirtana despite seeing no visible results.
Meeting the Hippies	In 1966 the Lower East Side of New York was filled with young people seeking spiritual alternatives to mainstream society[citation:6]. Prabhupada began attracting a few of these hippies to his small storefront. They were drawn by his kirtana which they said was better than LSD. He told them "Chant Hare Krishna and stay high forever."
The Storefront at 26 Second Avenue	On July 11 1966 Prabhupada signed the lease for a small storefront at 26 Second Avenue in New York[citation:7]. The rent was two hundred thirty dollars per month. The space was small with a low ceiling and a linoleum floor. But for Prabhupada it was the first ISKCON temple the place where his society would be born. He called it a holy place of transcendental pastimes.
The First Kirtana	The first evening in the storefront Prabhupada sat before a picture of Lord Chaitanya and began chanting Hare Krishna on his harmonium[citation:7]. A few young people came in off the street and sat down. As he chanted one of them began to dance. Prabhupada smiled and told him "You are dancing just like Lord Chaitanya." That night the seed of sankirtana was planted in America.
Teaching How to Cook	In the early days Prabhupada would personally go into the small kitchen at the storefront to show his disciples how to prepare prasadam[citation:5]. He taught them that cooking was not a burden but a form of worship. "Only a lazy man cannot cook" he told them. He then told the story of the lazy men who would rather burn than get up from a fire.
The First Initiation	On September 9 1966 Prabhupada held the first initiations in ISKCON history[citation:7]. He gave spiritual names to his first disciples: Mukunda Janaki and Hayagriva. Each disciple promised to chant sixteen rounds of the Hare Krishna mantra daily and follow four regulative principles. Prabhupada personally chanted on their beads giving them the mercy of the disciplic succession.
Founding ISKCON	On July 13 1966 Srila Prabhupada officially registered the International Society for Krishna Consciousness in New York[citation:6]. He signed the incorporation papers at a small office in Manhattan. The society's purpose was to systematically propagate spiritual knowledge to society at large. Prabhupada was now sixty-nine years old and had a legally recognized movement.
The Marriage of Mukunda and Janaki	Soon after the first initiations Prabhupada performed the first wedding in ISKCON between his disciples Mukunda and Janaki[citation:7]. He taught them that marriage in Krishna consciousness is not for sense gratification but for mutual spiritual advancement. The couple would serve as preaching partners opening the first London center and spreading the movement across Europe.
The United Nations Kirtana	On August 31 1966 Prabhupada and his small group of disciples were invited to open the Peace Vigil at the United Nations Building[citation:7]. They chanted Hare Krishna in the delegates' lounge. The New York newspapers ran pictures of "Swamiji's group" at the UN. For the first time the holy name was heard in the halls of world diplomacy.
Tompkins Square Park	In October 1966 Prabhupada led a massive public kirtana in Tompkins Square Park on New York's Lower East Side[citation:7]. Hundreds of young people gathered to chant and dance. Newspapers covered the event describing the Swami in his dhoti leading the ecstatic chanting. This public harinama became the model for spreading Krishna consciousness in city streets worldwide.
The LSD Question	Many of the young people who came to Prabhupada were using LSD seeking spiritual experiences[citation:7]. Prabhupada told them LSD was not the answer. "No matter how high you go on LSD" he said "it reaches a peak and comes down. But spiritual consciousness keeps you high forever." He gave them a higher intoxication—the Hare Krishna mantra.
First Trip to San Francisco	In January 1967 Prabhupada traveled to San Francisco where a group of young people had heard his chanting[citation:10]. They invited him to stay and he began holding kirtanas in the Haight-Ashbury district the center of the hippie movement. Soon a temple was established and the San Francisco center became one of ISKCON's strongest preaching bases.
The Ratha Yatra in San Francisco	Prabhupada wanted to introduce the ancient festival of Ratha Yatra to America[citation:10]. In 1967 despite many obstacles his disciples pulled Lord Jagannatha's cart through the streets of San Francisco. Thousands of people saw the Deities for the first time. Prabhupada said this festival would bring Krishna consciousness to the West just as it had spread through India for thousands of years.
The First London Center	In 1968 Prabhupada sent his disciples Mukunda and Janaki to open a center in London[citation:6]. They started with a small storefront and began chanting in the streets. Within months they had attracted many young people including musicians and artists. Prabhupada visited London in 1969 and was overjoyed to see his movement taking root in the land where he had been ordered to preach.
Meeting George Harrison	While in London Prabhupada met George Harrison of the Beatles[citation:6]. Harrison was already chanting Hare Krishna and had produced a record of the mantra. He invited Prabhupada to his country estate and they discussed philosophy for hours. Harrison later donated a large estate for ISKCON's English headquarters at Bhaktivedanta Manor.
Writing Bhagavad-gita As It Is	Throughout 1967 Prabhupada worked on his translation and commentary of the Bhagavad-gita[citation:6]. He wrote late into the night often by kerosene lamp. The manuscript grew to nearly four hundred pages. He refused to compromise the pure message for academic acceptance. When Macmillan published the Gita in 1968 it became the standard edition used worldwide.
The Macmillan Deal	Prabhupada's disciples arranged a meeting with executives at Macmillan publishers in New York[citation:6]. Prabhupada presented his Bhagavad-gita manuscript. The executives were impressed and offered a contract. This was the first time a major Western publisher had agreed to distribute a translation of the Gita by a traditional Vaisnava acarya. Prabhupada saw it as Krishna's arrangement.
Stinson Beach Recovery	After suffering a mild stroke in San Francisco Prabhupada went to Stinson Beach to recover[citation:10]. The climate was foggy and damp. He stayed in a small house on the hill. Even while ill he continued initiating disciples. One nervous young man came for initiation and Prabhupada lovingly gave him the name Aniruddha. He taught that service must be steady regardless of external conditions.
The Return of Rabhindra Svarupa	A former disciple named Rabhindra Svarupa who had left the movement returned to Prabhupada in great distress[citation:10]. He crawled to Prabhupada's feet sobbing that he wanted to know God directly without a guru. Prabhupada gently stroked his head and said "Don't be a crazy fellow. Stay with us if possible." He then taught everyone the importance of humility and the necessity of the spiritual master.
The Los Angeles Command	As Prabhupada prepared to leave San Francisco for India his disciples wept at the airport[citation:10]. One hid his ticket joking "Now you cannot go." Prabhupada smiled and said "That is all right—I already have my boarding pass." Before boarding he gave a final instruction "I have a temple in New York Montreal and San Francisco but none in Los Angeles. Please preach there."
The First Los Angeles Temple	Inspired by Prabhupada's command his disciples Nandarani and Dayananda found a storefront on La Cienega Boulevard in Los Angeles[citation:10]. Within months the center was thriving. Prabhupada visited and was pleased to see kirtana and classes happening daily. Los Angeles eventually became the international headquarters of the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust the world's largest publisher of Vedic literature.
Preaching in Montreal	Prabhupada traveled to Montreal where he established a temple on Park Avenue[citation:6]. He would hold classes late into the night teaching the Bhagavatam to his eager disciples. The Montreal temple became a model for how to present Krishna consciousness in a French-speaking culture. Many of Prabhupada's most dedicated disciples came from this center.
The Boston Harvard Lecture	While visiting Boston Prabhupada was invited to speak at Harvard University[citation:6]. He lectured on the Bhagavad-gita before a packed auditorium of students and professors. He answered their sophisticated questions with clear logic and scriptural authority. The Harvard lecture demonstrated that Krishna consciousness could hold its own in the most intellectual circles.
The First World Tour	By 1970 Prabhupada had begun traveling around the world continuously[citation:6]. He visited centers in Europe the United States Canada and India. He would often say "I am a parivrajakacarya—a traveling teacher." He never stayed in one place for long. Every day was spent preaching writing or meeting with disciples and guests.
The Moscow Stopover	In 1971 Prabhupada traveled through Moscow on his way to India[citation:10]. Even in the communist Soviet Union where religion was suppressed he chanted Hare Krishna quietly in his hotel room. He prayed that one day the holy name would be heard in every town and village of the world including Russia. Years later after the fall of communism ISKCON established a strong presence there.
The Land Is Yours	In 1971 Prabhupada sent two disciples Tamala Krishna and Bali-mardana to purchase land in Mayapur[citation:8]. Days passed with no news. Prabhupada waited anxiously pacing his room in Calcutta. Finally late one night Tamala Krishna returned and prostrated himself. "So what is your news?" Prabhupada asked. Tamala Krishna looked up triumphantly and said "The land is yours!" Prabhupada leaned back with a deep sigh of relief.
The Mayapur Vision	Prabhupada envisioned a magnificent temple in Mayapur the birthplace of Lord Chaitanya[citation:8]. He told his architects the main temple should be more than three hundred feet high—taller than the United States Capitol. Inside would be a three-dimensional model of the universe based on the Srimad Bhagavatam. This Mayapur Chandrodaya Mandir would be the world headquarters of ISKCON.
The Bengali Widow's Service	While living in Vrindavana Prabhupada observed a poor Bengali widow who each morning before dawn walked to the Yamuna River to fetch water for the Radha-Damodara Deities[citation:2]. Even in the cold winter she never missed a day. Prabhupada told his disciples "For this simple service she will return back to Godhead. One who cannot rise early is not serious about spiritual life."
The Haunted House of Lucknow	Prabhupada once rented a house in Lucknow that was reputed to be haunted by ghosts[citation:5]. When his disciples expressed concern he laughed and said "I am not afraid of ghosts—I am ghostproof." He then explained that ghosts are simply sinful souls in subtle bodies and that chanting Hare Krishna drives them away. He had no fear because he was always absorbed in Krishna.
The Bombay Temple Battle	Prabhupada struggled for years to establish a large temple in Bombay[citation:5]. He called the city his "office" while Vrindavana was his "home". Despite legal battles financial obstacles and political opposition he remained determined. He told his disciples "No one listens to a poor man" and used this as motivation to build a magnificent temple that would command respect for Lord Krishna.
Teaching the Goswami Mood	In Madras in 1972 a three-year-old girl named Sarasvati was given a silver Krishna murti which Prabhupada took from her to teach a lesson[citation:3]. She cried searching everywhere for Krishna. Finally Prabhupada revealed that he had the murti. He then explained to the adults that this mood of separation from Krishna is the highest form of devotion. "Prabhupada has Krishna" the girl exclaimed capturing the truth of the spiritual master's position.
The African Preaching	Prabhupada traveled to Nairobi Kenya in 1972 to preach to both Indians and native Africans[citation:4]. When a journalist asked his special message for Kenya he replied "I have no new method for a new place. The message is the same—universal." He led kirtanas in front of African crowds who could not understand his words but loved the chanting. Over two thousand students came to hear him at the University of Nairobi.
The Deerskin Lesson	While in Africa a devotee offered Prabhupada a deerskin to sit on[citation:4]. Prabhupada became furious. "Vaishnavas do not use this" he said. "This is for yogis who meditate. A Vaishnava serves Krishna he does not sit on animal skins." He taught that every detail of a devotee's life must reflect the mood of service not imitation of other paths.
The Ghosts of Mayapur	Someone once asked Prabhupada about ghosts in Mayapur[citation:5]. He replied "In Mayapur there were Muhammadan ghosts but not anymore. By our chanting Hare Krishna ghosts are driven away." He explained that the holy dhama is purifying and that sankirtana cleanses the atmosphere of all inauspicious influences. The holy name is so powerful it can deliver even ghostly beings.
The Final Return to Vrindavana	In May 1977 Prabhupada was in Rishikesh when a violent storm struck[citation:5]. His health suddenly declined. He told his secretary "I want to leave immediately for Vrindavana. If it is time for me to pass away let it be in Vrindavana." They drove through the night and arrived at the Krishna-Balarama temple where he had made his home.
Arriving at the Krishna-Balarama Temple	When Prabhupada arrived in Vrindavana he saw for the first time the stone marker reading "Bhaktivedanta Swami Marg"[citation:5]. The devotees carried him on a palanquin to the temple where he offered respects to the Deities. He then spoke to the assembled devotees "Whatever I have to say I have spoken in my books. Now try to understand it and continue your endeavors."
I Have Done My Part	In those final days Prabhupada told his disciples "I have done my part"[citation:5]. He explained that as Krishna lives eternally so the living being lives eternally. "One who has done service to the Lord lives forever. So live forever by serving Krishna." He was peaceful and without fear having accomplished his spiritual master's order.
The Temple of Understanding	Prabhupada continued to plan even in his final weeks. He described the great temple in Mayapur with its universe model and the Deities of Radha-Krishna in a jeweled palace. He told the architects this was not a dream but a vision given by the previous acaryas. Though he would not see it completed his disciples would fulfill his desire.
The Cucumber Lesson	On the road from Rishikesh to Delhi Prabhupada saw a man selling cucumbers[citation:5]. He asked the driver to stop. "Cucumbers are good for quenching thirst" he said. Even in his weakened condition he was practical and aware of his surroundings. He took a small piece and then continued the journey absorbed in thoughts of Krishna and his service.
Eating Is Finished	While resting in Delhi Prabhupada was offered a large plate of Deity prasadam but he only took a few tastes[citation:5]. He told his disciples "Eating is finished. I prayed to Krishna to be freed from eating and sleeping and it is happening. I have already given up mating and defending. Now all these material activities are finished with."
The Last Initiation	Even in his final months Prabhupada continued initiating new disciples[citation:10]. He would personally chant on their beads and give them spiritual names. One young man who was desperate to be initiated was brought to Prabhupada who comforted him gave him the name Jivananda and smiled. That disciple later became a leading sankirtana devotee.
The Householder Disciple	In Vrindavana a retired family man named Mr. Bose came to see Prabhupada[citation:5]. He expressed his desire to live at the temple. Prabhupada was honest with him "You will not be able to adjust to this way of life" he said. But when Mr. Bose expressed determination Prabhupada encouraged him "You have a very good family so it is hopeful."
The Ghostproof Swami	When the conversation turned to ghosts Prabhupada told stories of haunted houses he had lived in[citation:5]. He explained that ghosts are evil entities that can sometimes be seen entering latrines or sitting on pillars. But he said "I am not afraid of ghosts. I am ghostproof." By chanting Hare Krishna he said all inauspicious influences are driven away.
The Morning Rides	During his final days in Vrindavana Prabhupada would ride in a car each morning down Bhaktivedanta Swami Marg[citation:5]. The fresh morning air was cool compared to the heat of the day. The road was lined with nim trees and shrubs. He would then return and lovingly behold the Deities of Krishna and Balarama before resting.
Becoming the Eyes	Even while ill Prabhupada remained involved in managing his movement[citation:5]. He told his secretary Tamala Krishna "You become my eyes." He wanted to know everything that was happening in ISKCON around the world. But he also asked for complete relief from management saying it was too much botheration. He wanted to leave his disciples to carry on without him.
The Unfinished Bombay Temple	The unfinished Bombay temple disturbed Prabhupada very much[citation:5]. He told Tamala Krishna "If the Deities are not properly installed in my presence it will be a great shock." He had worked so hard to get that project accomplished. He prayed that Krishna would allow him to see the temple completed before he left the world.
The Power of Early Rising	Prabhupada stressed the importance of rising early in the morning for spiritual life[citation:2]. He told the story of the Bengali widow who never missed fetching water for the Deities even in the cold Vrindavana winter. "One must rise before brahma-muhurta" he said. "That is very auspicious. One will take the trouble to do so if one is serious about spiritual life."
The Quality of a Disciple	Prabhupada taught that a disciple must always be ready to serve the spiritual master without hesitation[citation:10]. He told his disciples "You must always be ready for my call." Service requires steadiness and presence not just enthusiasm when convenient. A disciple who is always available for service will make rapid advancement.
The Sloka for the Disciples	In his final days Prabhupada recited a Sanskrit verse glorifying Krishna as the son of Devaki the light of the Vrishni dynasty whose form brings liberation to the world[citation:10]. He reminded his disciples of the great blessing of directly hearing such realizations from the spiritual master. This direct association was rare and precious.
The Telegram to Delhi	Before leaving America for the last time Prabhupada telegraphed Sri Krishna Pandit in Delhi to prepare his quarters at the Radha-Krishna temple[citation:10]. Even in illness he was meticulous planning his travel and accommodations. He then planned to go to Vrindavana for rest and spiritual solace. His letters continued to show attention to ISKCON's affairs.
The Brijbasi Correspondence	Even from his sickbed Prabhupada corresponded about finances and printing with the Brijbasi Company of Mathura[citation:10]. He never stopped managing his book publication. The printing and distribution of his books was his life's blood. He wanted the books to continue going out to the world even after his departure.
The Real Children	Before leaving San Francisco for the last time Prabhupada told his disciples "I have a few children in India but you are my real children. Now I am going to India for a little while"[citation:10]. He loved his Western disciples deeply considering them his true family because they had dedicated their lives to spreading Krishna consciousness.
The Steady Determination	When a young nervous boy came for initiation Prabhupada named him Aniruddha meaning "unconquerable"[citation:10]. He taught that a devotee must be steady and determined. The spiritual life is not for the fickle or weak-hearted. One must remain fixed in service through all difficulties and doubts.
The Transparent Via Medium	When Rabhindra Svarupa thought he could approach God directly without a guru Prabhupada corrected him[citation:10]. "Without the spiritual master as a transparent via medium" he said "one cannot know Krishna." The guru is not an obstacle to God but the lens through which God is seen clearly. This is the secret of disciplic succession.
The Hare Krishna in Moscow	Traveling through Moscow Prabhupada chanted Hare Krishna quietly in his hotel room[citation:10]. Even under communist rule he was determined to spread the holy name. He prayed that one day the mantra would be heard openly in Russia. His prayer was answered decades later when ISKCON established temples throughout the former Soviet Union.
The Value of Association	Prabhupada often said that association with devotees is the most important factor for spiritual progress[citation:10]. He gave his disciples the blessing of close association with the spiritual master where they could directly hear realizations from the scriptures. This association even for a short time could change one's life forever.
The Final Kirtana	At his last San Francisco kirtana the devotees sensed it might be their final meeting with their spiritual master[citation:10]. Many begged for initiation before he departed. Though frail Prabhupada personally initiated as many as possible even when advised to stop. He spoke gently to them about his mission and their role in continuing it.
The Wailing Disciple	At the airport as Prabhupada prepared to leave for India many devotees wept openly[citation:10]. One disciple hid his ticket as a playful gesture to keep him in America. Prabhupada simply smiled and said "That is all right—I already have my boarding pass. I am Indian. They will let me into my own country."
The Los Angeles Prediction	Before leaving America for the last time Prabhupada told his disciples to open a temple in Los Angeles[citation:10]. Within months they had a storefront. Los Angeles later became the headquarters of the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust the world's largest publisher of Vedic books. Prabhupada's instruction had borne tremendous fruit.
The Vrindavana Home	Prabhupada always considered Vrindavana his real home[citation:5]. On the road from Delhi to Vrindavana Tamala Krishna noticed he seemed more peaceful. "You look very happy to be going to Vrindavana" he said. Prabhupada replied "Yes Vrindavana is my home and Bombay is my office." He had spent his most productive writing years in Vrindavana.
The Ten Million Books	By the time of his departure Prabhupada's Bhaktivedanta Book Trust had published millions of his books in dozens of languages[citation:6]. He had single-handedly translated over sixty volumes of Vedic literature including the Srimad Bhagavatam the Caitanya-caritamrta and the Bhagavad-gita As It Is. No other Vaisnava acarya in history had produced so much literature for the West.
The Seven Volumes of Lilamrita	Srila Prabhupada's biography Srila Prabhupada-lilamrita written by his disciple Satsvarupa Dasa Goswami runs to seven volumes[citation:1]. It covers his life from childhood through his final days. The volumes contain detailed accounts of his preaching struggles and triumphs. It remains the definitive biography of ISKCON's founder-acarya.
The One Hundred Eight Temples	By the time of Prabhupada's departure ISKCON had grown from a single storefront in New York to over one hundred temples worldwide[citation:6]. Centers existed on every continent. Prabhupada's vision of a worldwide society of Krishna devotees had been realized in just eleven short years.
The Legacy of the Spiritual Master	Srila Prabhupada departed from this world on November 14 1977 in Vrindavana[citation:5]. His body was placed in samadhi at the Krishna-Balarama temple where he had prayed to leave the world. His movement continues to grow with thousands of temples farm communities and restaurants around the globe. His books are read by millions. His spiritual master's order was fulfilled.
The Humble Beginnings	When asked how he had succeeded Prabhupada would always give credit to his spiritual master and to Krishna[citation:6]. He said "I simply tried to carry out the order of my spiritual master. I had no qualification but he asked me to do it so I tried." From a Bowery room to a worldwide movement—this is the power of pure devotion.
The Unflinching Determination	Throughout his life Prabhupada never gave up despite every obstacle[citation:6]. Poverty old age illness rejection and loneliness could not stop him. He would often say "My spiritual master told me to preach in English to the Western world. I am trying to do it. Whether I succeed or fail I am simply trying." That unflinching determination changed the world.
